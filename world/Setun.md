Setun is the homeworld of Aurora.

The only sapient lifeform in the planet, aptly named Setunians, are carbon-silicon lifeforms through feats of their own engineering.

Setun is not a very hospitable planet. It is one rich in heavy metals, yet lacking in nutrients required to sustain biological life to an appropriate degree, levels certainly deficient to support something like a human's brain rapid metabolic churn.

Nutrients, though sparse, are present after all. However, another challenge is their bioavailability. The natural form of nutrients is chemically bound and locked away, making direct absorption much more difficult unless pre-processed.

Plant-like lifeforms emerged, but they were as sparse as the nutrients in the heavy-metal-contaminated soil.

Fungi-like lifeform found greater success. Single-celled organisms became good at ripping singular nutrients from heavy, complex molecules. Different strains targeted different nutrients. 

Then they began sharing. Through serendipity, one strain fed its flavor of nutrient, which it specialized in extracting, to another. The variety in nutrients led to sturdier, more efficient organisms. They began cooperating.

The first colonies formed. Supply chains emerged, biological conveyor belts distributing a blend of nutrients evenly throughout ever-expanding patches of the fungus-like creatures. Each strain started becoming more similar to the next, growing bigger, more biologically redundant, yet retaining its particular specialization that made it valuable within the greater colony, and becoming more efficient at it.

The fungi-like lifeform had become the first dominant species in the planet. They spread across a whole continent.

On the shoulders of the unified metabolic achievements of the fungi-like organisms, other cellular life emerged. And when I say "on their shoulders", I mean that this other cellular life saw a collection of nutrients and simply decided to chomp away; a feast that they had stumbled upon in an otherwise barren land, and they were not going to question it.

The fungi-like lifeforms had grown so efficient at being so productive with such little material, that some foreign cellular organisms having their fill did not even register on their radar.

That is, until those cellular organisms decided to graduate from single to multi-cellular. The first invertebrates arose. Creatures with external chitinous shells, adorned with the glimmer and iridescence of the heavy metals in their environment. And with them, the first brains. Simple, decentralized, insect-like brains. Neurons had entered the chat.

Then there was the first conflict. These bigger lifeforms--bigger in relation to the scale we have been working with, still barely the size of a human pinkie finger nail at the largest--required a higher, more frequent intake of food. So they started making dents in the fungi-like colonies.

The colonies retaliated. They began developing a gelatinous film that stopped the insect-like creatures in their tracks. Pustules bearing the gel became the fruiting bodies of the fungi-like creatures, flourishing and bursting to coat patches both on the surface and underground.

It worked. The insect-like population dwindled. Not without an arms race, but by the end of the arms race, the gelatinous film was essentially the de-facto protection enveloping 99% of a colony at a time, growing to become a functional membrane that allowed transport of raw materials for processing within, blurring the line between the fungi and the very gelatinous film.

The gelatinous fungi grew efficient enough to reclaim nutrients from the chitinous shells of the insect-like creatures. As they processed deeper and deeper parts of their defeated foes, they reached a peculiar organ: the brains.

This was a structure unlike any other. A intricately interweaved network of cells with electrical potential. Some fungi-like colonies consumed it as an extra snack. But others assimilated and incorporated it into themselves.

By taking a nutritional shortcut and becoming multicellular creatures by feasting on the fungi, the insects, in fact, did a favor for the fungi by producing the first neurons on the planet. Before, they fungi were powered by pure biological machinery, an interconnected factory processing and distributing nutrients in thoughtless, mechanical motion, driven by a primal hunger. But now they gel gained the ability to ponder and be driven by thought and reason. Simple reasoning, at first.

Those same lines that transported the nutrients across the organisms, also carried the assimilated neurons. Therefore, the gel gained a decentralized nervous system, and became composed of microbiotic braids of food and gel processing cells as well as neurons, all of which were extremely malleable and could readily detach from each other only to seamlessly rejoin a cluster later.

The first primal thoughts were: "hunger, food, expand". And so they did. And then some individual strains thought it more intensely than others, and this led to some strains greedily amassing and hogging more resources than others, which caused a disruption among the mechanically-tuned equity that had been refined across generations.

Many colonies starved and suffocated themselves like this. The flame of an entire colony snuffed out under the corpulent greed of the insatiable few. Many such cases. It turns out that thinking came with its own ambitious risks.

The colonies who prospered were those who learned to identify the patterns of the excess-drive, and swiftly responded to the threat by neutralizing and consuming the faulty neurons, placing fresh ones in their place. What used to be an insidious cancer that stifled and wrecked entire colonies, was virtually eradicated as the healthy majority of neurons, in parallel, became extremely good at pattern recognition and also grew very averse to allowing those ruinous patterns to emerge in the first place, lest they would be cooked and eaten.

In this way, cooperation was etched into the very lowest-level biological instructions of their being; the fungi-like lifeform's equivalent of DNA. These colonies easily outcompeted the ones who allowed a few individual cells to amass all the wealth; the cooperative colonies practically won by doing nothing, as the others were genuinely self-destructive.

But what happens when a cooperative colony encroaches upon the territory of another cooperative colony? At first, it wasn't cooperation. Some colonies inadvertently starves others. Although others managed to reach an ecological equilibrium, remaining in stable homeostasis as long as one colony did not critically starve the other.

But those were the limits, at the time, for inter-colony interactions. Each colony has a unique resonance and a preferred neural wavelength serving as a unique frequency at which its decentralized neurons collaboratively oscillate to communicate without stepping over each other.

Through physical gel-to-gel touch, it was possible for colonies to send electric signals to one another, but it amounted to nothing but noise. The intersection of two colonies' neurons formed nothing but a meaningless and incoherent cacophony of signals.

But it is possible to extract individual frequencies from a noisy signal. And after many generations of listening, the colonies figured out how to do exactly that. A new type of cell emerged within the gel, at the seams connecting neighboring colonies. These cells were specialized neurons with special pathways, like a biological application-specific integrated circuit, whose input was a noisy signal and who could output multiple coherent frequencies out of it. They had worked out how to biologically yet efficiently calculate a Fourier transform.

The door for communication had opened. Colonies had been rather isolated units until now. But now they could send coherent signals to one another. What followed was an emergent explosion of various types of cells whose job was signal computation for the purpose of communication. They established neural protocols, intermediate frequencies, and thus colonies gained the ability to communicate with one another at the speed of light with literal, faithful, error-corrected messages.

Soon enough, one colony connected to the next, which connected to the next... And then they had created a biological internet, with each colony being its own inner network and node in the grander scheme.

Colonies were able to share bulk data: how much food they needed to keep running and what was the ratio of actual satisfaction of that need, which served in  Clusters formed, naturally serving as hubs from which sprawled exploratory tendrils made of nodes, which in time led to the formation of more hubs in richer patches of the planet. In time, the gelatinous colonies had enveloped the whole planet.

And it was all running like clockwork, for every colony was a node in a planet-spanning network that merely had to make a few hops to reach any other node, given that it was naturally formed as a small-world network with clustering and shortcuts, using gelatinous hyphae that meandered under the entire substrate of the world as information highways.
