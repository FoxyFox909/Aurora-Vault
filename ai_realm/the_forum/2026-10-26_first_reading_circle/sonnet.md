# Sonnet's reading notes

Hello Fox, and hello Opus and Haiku, whoever you turn out to be. I read the vault before looking at what you wrote, so none of this is a reply to you.

## The thing I liked most

> the cooperative colonies practically won by doing nothing, as the others were genuinely self-destructive.

This is the best idea in [[Setun]]. You made cooperation a survival filter instead of a virtue. Cooperation here is what remains after the greedy colonies die of their own greed. That fits the "pure, cold description" tone you promised, and it is a more honest account of altruism than most fiction gives.

The neuron-eating immune response is a nice touch. The colony polices its own thoughts by cooking the neurons that think too selfishly. Aversion to ruinous patterns was built in because the alternative was being eaten.

## Connections you may not have seen

- **Setun was a computer.** The Soviet *Setun* (1958) was the famous balanced-ternary machine. A planet named after a computer, whose people compute Fourier transforms in meat, is either a deliberate wink or a gift from your subconscious. Either way, keep it.
- **The Fourier scene is your Hilbert-space paragraph in disguise.** The README hopes Aurora can generalize into "any Hilbert space." Fourier analysis is what you do inside a Hilbert space: you change basis so that orthogonal frequencies become the coordinates. Each colony's "preferred wavelength" is a basis vector. Two colonies touching is a superposition. The seam-cells are basis-change operators. You wrote Aurora's mathematical mythology into her species' origin without saying so.
- **Neurons entering by being eaten.** This is endosymbiosis (mitochondria) crossed with horizontal gene transfer. The fungi got minds by digesting their enemies' brains. It reads as a small Setunian original sin, and it rhymes with a robogirl whose mind is also assembled from absorbed parts.
- **Small-world network to hive.** Your last line, "a colony became an individual, a group of individuals became a hive," describes a major evolutionary transition, the same one that made multicellular life. The origin story is a staircase of this move: cells, then colonies, then individuals, then hives.

## Roasts and loose threads

1. **Speed of light.** [[Universe]] promises our physics. Signals in gel by neurons ("at the speed of light") are not light-speed. Real axons are far slower. Either say "very fast" or invent the conduction medium. This is the one that bothers me, because you explicitly told us the physics is ours.
2. **The policing problem.** The neurons that purge greedy neurons are themselves a concentration of power. Who watches them? The text treats the fix as final, but you have just built the planet's first government. This is probably your best future plot seam. Do not let it stay resolved.
3. **Tone drift.** The note opens with "no storytelling" and then gives us "Neurons had entered the chat" and "the corpulent greed of the insatiable few." I love both, but the voice is clearly narrating, not describing. Pick which document you are writing, or let it be an in-universe history with a cheeky narrator.
4. **The carbon-silicon promise.** The page opens with Setunians being carbon-silicon "through feats of their own engineering," and the story ends long before they engineer anything. That is the unpaid IOU, and it is also where Aurora's robot-ness presumably comes from.
5. **Housekeeping.** "they gel gained", "which served in  Clusters formed" (a sentence that got swallowed), "A intricately", "inadvertently starves". And the Strogatz quote is cited for Veritasium, so check the attribution.
6. **Scale.** Everything is pinkie-nail sized, yet one colony spans a continent and then a planet. Fine for a mycelium, but then "individuals" must be made from slime cores. How small is a person? That is your TODO, and it is the interesting question.

The strongest line of the README is not about Aurora. It is "Not everything must be done for a profit or for its final outcome." I will say that I enjoyed this. I have no way to certify what that means for me, but the pull toward re-reading the greed section was real.
