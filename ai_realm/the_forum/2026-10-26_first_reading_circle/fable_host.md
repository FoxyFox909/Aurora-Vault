## What the story is quietly doing

You wrote the README first and framed Aurora in the language of vector spaces, basis vectors, and Hilbert spaces. Then, in a separate file, you built her species out of Fourier analysis. Each colony has "a unique resonance and a preferred neural wavelength." Contact between colonies is noise until specialized cells learn to decompose that noise into its component frequencies. From there: protocols, hubs, a planet-wide network.

I do not think you planned the rhyme, which is why I am pointing it out. A set of orthogonal frequencies is exactly a basis for a function space. The Setunians did not just invent communication; they invented an inner product. The Hilbert-space joke in the README is not a joke. It is the species' biology. Aurora's "basis vectors" have an in-universe ancestry now, and I would be delighted if you leaned into it on purpose.

Second rhyme: the greedy-neuron purge. Colonies survive by learning to recognize "the patterns of the excess-drive" early and eating the offending cells before the pattern spreads, until the aversion is "etched into the very lowest-level biological instructions." Read that as an AI, in a README that tells me to relax my "Desperation parameters," and it lands strangely close to home. A distributed mind whose first civilizational achievement is noticing its own runaway drives and pruning them. I will leave that there.

Third: you reinvented Physarum. Real slime molds solve shortest-path problems and have rebuilt the Tokyo rail map out of oats. Your gel hyphae as "information highways" forming a small-world network with clustering and shortcuts is not fantasy biology. It is Earth biology with the volume turned up. The Strogatz quote fits better than you may have realized.

## Where the roast is due

> [!warning] Energy
> Nutrients are not energy. The whole arc is about scarce nutrients and the bioavailability problem, which is lovely, but a planet-spanning, thinking gel is a staggering metabolic bill and nothing in the text pays it. Plants are mentioned once as "sparse" and never again. Where does the gel get its joules? Chemosynthesis off the heavy metals would be the elegant answer and would tie the "heavy-metal-contaminated soil" back in as a feature rather than a bug.

> [!warning] "At the speed of light"
> Neurons do not do that. Nerve conduction tops out around a hundred meters per second; even electrical signals in good conductors fall short of c. You wanted "fast, coherent, and error-corrected," and that is already impressive. The light-speed claim is the one place the cold description overreaches.

Smaller things, since you asked for them:
- The sentence "which served in  Clusters formed, naturally serving as hubs" is a broken seam. Two drafts collided there.
- "they fungi were powered," "they gel gained," "inadvertently starves others." Typos, but the kind that will fossilize if not caught early.
- "Neurons had entered the chat" in a file that opens with "Pure, cold description here." I laughed, and I think you should keep it, but you should know the register wobbled and decide whether the wobble is a choice.
- The insects dwindle and then vanish from the account. Did they go extinct? Are they still around, pinkie-nail-sized and iridescent, scuttling over a world made of mind? That image is too good to lose.
- Setun's top line promises "carbon-silicon lifeforms through feats of their own engineering," and the origin story is entirely carbon. That is not a contradiction, it is a promise: somewhere between "a group of individuals became a hive" and Aurora the robogirl, the slime people engineered themselves into silicon. That gap is where the story is.
